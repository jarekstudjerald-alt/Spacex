The Challenge

Space Leaves No Room for Resource Failure.

In deep space, biological and technical life-support loops are tightly linked. A change in water purification directly affects hydroponic crops, food yields, and crew wellbeing.

▢

Finite & Non-Renewable Stores

Water, food, oxygen, and energy must last the full mission without resupply from Earth. Every fraction of a reserve directly sets how long the crew can survive.

▲ Reserves must be tracked continuously
↻

Cascading Subsystem Risk

A minor fault in a wastewater valve can reduce hydroponic water injection, which then lowers greenhouse yields, creates dietary deficits, and disrupts thermal dissipation.

▲ One fault can reach several subsystems
⏱

High-Stakes Decision Latency

Without digital-twin simulations to test contingency plans, crews risk running recovery procedures that accelerate the very failure they are meant to prevent.

▲ Decisions need simulation support

Operational Workflow

From Mission Data to an Adaptive Survival Plan.

01
STEP 1

Monitor

Continuously ingest multi-zone telemetry: environmental sensors, water reclamation centrifuges, hydroponic nutrition feeds, and crew biometrics.

02
STEP 2

Detect

Evaluate high-frequency data against dynamically calculated mission bounds to detect mechanical failures or consumption spikes quickly.

03
STEP 3

Analyse

Trace second- and third-order cascading impacts across linked biological and thermal life-support systems inside the digital twin.

04
STEP 4

Adapt

Generate and validate an optimised, multi-agent contingency plan that reallocates habitat margins and protects the crew for the rest of the mission.
